    Step-by-step guide to converting PDF to JPG

    Pick your pages

    Leave All pages selected to convert the whole file, or choose Custom pages and type numbers and ranges separated by commas. 1-3, 7, 10- means pages 1 to 3, page 7, and page 10 through to the end.

    Choose a resolution

    72 DPI gives small images suited to web thumbnails. 150 DPI is a good default for screens and slides. 300 DPI is sharper for printing but makes bigger files. A US Letter page at 300 DPI is 2550 by 3300 pixels.

    Set the quality

    92% is a sensible default. Drop to 75% to save space on photo-heavy pages. Text-heavy pages show JPG artifacts around letters sooner than photos do, so keep quality high for documents.

    Convert and download

    Click Convert to JPG. The first run downloads the PDF engine, then pages are rendered one at a time with a progress bar. Save images individually or use the ZIP button.

    Expert tips and common mistakes

    • Don't use 300 DPI for the web. It makes large files with no visible gain on a screen.
    • Convert only what you need. A 60-page PDF at 300 DPI is a lot of data for your device.
    • Expect no selectable text. A JPG is a picture. For text you can copy, use Image to Text Converter on the result.
    • Check the page numbers. They're the physical position in the file, not the printed numbers.
    • Use a higher DPI for small print. Fine text blurs at 72 DPI.
    • Keep the original PDF. JPG pages can't be turned back into live text.

    Troubleshooting

    • "That file is not a PDF." Re-export the document as a PDF from its original app.
    • "Password-protected" message. Remove the password in a viewer you trust, save a copy, then load it here.
    • The converter engine won't load. Check your connection, or whether an ad blocker or network filter blocks cdn.jsdelivr.net.
    • "Page X is outside this PDF." Check the page count shown after conversion starts.
    • Images look blurry. Use 150 or 300 DPI and a higher quality setting.
    • The tab runs out of memory. Convert fewer pages at a time, or lower the DPI.
    • A page renders blank or odd. Some PDFs use unusual features. Re-save the PDF from your viewer and try again.

    Glossary

    DPI
    Dots per inch. Higher DPI means more pixels and more detail.
    JPG (JPEG)
    A lossy image format. Lower quality means a smaller file.
    Rendering
    Drawing a PDF page as pixels.
    Megapixel
    One million pixels, a way to measure image size.
    ZIP archive
    A single file bundling several files together.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this PDF to JPG converter really free?

    Yes. You can convert as many PDFs as you like with no payment, account or daily quota, and the images carry no watermark. The limits are technical: PDFs up to 100 MB and up to 100 pages per run, because rendering happens in your browser's memory rather than on a server.

    Is it safe to convert a PDF to JPG here?

    Your PDF is processed in your browser and is never uploaded. Only the pdf.js engine files are downloaded from the jsDelivr CDN when you click Convert. Your document is read and rendered on your own device, which suits private paperwork, though you should avoid shared computers.

    Do I need to sign up or install anything?

    No. Open the page, drop in a PDF and convert it. There's no signup, no email prompt and no software to install. It works in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop and mobile, as long as your device has enough free memory.

    What's the file size limit?

    The tool accepts PDFs up to 100 MB and converts up to 100 pages per run. Each page is capped at about 16 megapixels, so oversized pages are scaled down slightly. For a longer document, use custom page numbers and convert it in batches.

    Does converting PDF to JPG reduce quality?

    Yes, to a degree. JPG is a lossy format, and the PDF's text becomes pixels. At 150 or 300 DPI with quality at 90% or above, pages look clean on screen and in print. Lower DPI or quality gives smaller files but softer text.

    Which resolution should I choose?

    Use 72 DPI for small web previews, 150 DPI for general screen use and slides, and 300 DPI for printing. Higher DPI produces larger images and takes longer, so don't use 300 DPI for the web. If text looks blurry, step up one level.

    Can I convert only some pages of my PDF?

    Yes. Choose Custom pages and type page numbers and ranges, like 1-3, 5, 8-. An open-ended range such as 8- runs from page 8 to the last page. Page numbers refer to the position in the file, not the printed numbers on the pages.

    Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

    Not directly. If a PDF needs a password to open, the tool shows a message instead of trying to open it. Remove the protection in a viewer you trust, if you're allowed to, save a copy, and then load that copy here.

    Can I convert a PDF to JPG on my phone?

    Yes. Tap the drop area, pick a PDF from your files or cloud storage, and use the same options as on desktop. For long documents on older phones, use a lower DPI or convert fewer pages at a time to avoid running out of memory.

    Does it work offline?

    Not on the first conversion. The page downloads the pdf.js engine, about 1.4 MB, from a CDN when you click Convert, so you need a connection at that moment. After that, your PDF is processed locally, and nothing about your file is sent anywhere.
